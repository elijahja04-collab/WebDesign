Understanding AI's carbon footprint

AI runs on data centers, which are large buildings full of computers that need electricity to operate. Electricity is used to train AI models and to answer prompts inputed to AI. Currenty, data centers are using about 1.5% of the world's electricity. This is seemingly small, but it is expected to grow in coming years. Researchers estimate data centers could reach 3% by 2030. The impact is also globally uneven, with 5% of electricity in the US going to data centers while in Ireland it is more than 20%.

Source: Our World in Data

Data centers also need water. AI computers heat up with use, and many data centers use water cooling. Globablly, data centers used an estimated 222 billion liters of water for cooling in 2025 alone. Compared to other industries, data centers use a small share of total water, but it can matter in dry regions where water is already scarce. The good news is newer cooling systems can use less water, and powering data centers with solar/wind energy can reduce water usage as well.

Sources: Live Science, Harvard Salata Institute
